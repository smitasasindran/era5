1 · Strategy catalogue

4 pipeline stages, 19 discrete strategies

The full cleaning pipeline breaks into 4 major stages, built from 19 discrete techniques: Normalization, Quality Filtering, Deduplication, and the PII Scrubber. Two stages are implemented and verified below; the other two are scoped and planned.

1 · Normalization 8 strategies Built

  • NFC Unicode normalization
  • Strip C0 / C1 control characters
  • Strip zero-width noise (ZWSP), BOM, bidi overrides
  • HTML entity unescaping
  • Strip the U+FFFD replacement character
  • Collapse whitespace runs
  • Flag ghost special tokens instead of deleting them
  • Preserve Brahmic joiners (ZWNJ / ZWJ) — always

4 · Quality Filtering 4 strategies Planned

  • Deterministic heuristics — mean word length, symbol-to-word ratio, Lines end in . !?, Duplicate-line fraction, Top 2-gram fraction, Common stop-words, Bullet-line ratio, Ellipsis-line ratio, Document word count
  • ML classifier, distilled from a large model to a cheap ~95%-accurate copy
  • Language-aware rules — e.g. Sanskrit has no full stop
  • Synthetic / rephrased data as a quality lever

5 · Deduplication 4 strategies Planned

  • Shingling — overlapping token windows (e.g. 50-token overlap)
  • Similarity-preserving hashing (MinHash) per shingle
  • Banding — hashes split into chunks for cheap comparison
  • Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) candidate lookup

6 · PII Scrubber 3 strategies Planned

  • Regex layer — emails, phone numbers, network addresses
  • NER / name-detection layer
  • Source-aware policy — keep for news, scrub for Reddit

3 · What was cleaned — Extraction

Pulling the article out of the page

Web-scraped text fields often still carry the page around the article: nav bars, cookie banners, footers — and it's exactly this leftover HTML that was producing ghost-token noise (<h3>, <video>) downstream. Extraction runs three tiers in order — trafilatura for genuine HTML pages, a BeautifulSoup structural pass (drop nav/footer/script/ table, and anything whose class/id names a cookie banner or sidebar), then a regex fallback for text with just a few leftover inline tags — so nothing is ever dropped outright, only degraded gracefully.

4 · Any other strategy or concern cleaned up

Six additional techniques applied

Found and fixed while actually running the pipeline against real data.

01

Extraction stage

Added as stage 0 after ghost-token noise was traced back to un-stripped HTML tags.

02

Mojibake repair (ftfy)

NFC alone can't fix double-encoded UTF-8 — every mojibake character is already "validly" encoded, just the wrong one.

03

De-hyphenation

Rejoins PDF/OCR-style line-wrapped words ("exam-\nple" → "example"), prose only — never touches code.

04

<table> dropped, not flattened

Flattening a table with get_text() jumbles every cell together — worse noise than just dropping it.

05

Ghost-token vocabulary restricted

An unrestricted pattern also matched HTML tags (<h3>), acronyms ([ERISA]), and even bracketed plurals (word[s]) — narrowed to a curated conversation-role vocabulary.

06

Script-purity diagnostic

Text-only signal (no language label needed) that catches legacy-font mojibake and language-switching — see the chart above.
